Blank Cell Guide

Why blank, dash, missing, and not-shown content cannot mean no, zero, or not applicable.

A blank is an absence, not a value

An empty cell shows nothing. That nothing is easy to mistake for a meaning — no, zero, none, not applicable — but an absence of information is not information. Treating a blank as a value quietly invents data that was never there.

What a blank can be

  • The cell currently shows nothing.
  • The value is missing or not displayed.
  • A dash standing for an empty or withheld entry.
  • Content that is present on the source but not shown in the sheet.

What a blank cannot be

  • Not a no, a zero, or a none.
  • Not a “not applicable” or “not included.”
  • Not a value you are free to invent to complete the row.

How to handle a blank

  1. Mark it not shown. Keep the cell visually blank or labeled as not shown.
  2. Do not fill it. Leave it open rather than writing a guessed value into the row.
  3. Re-check the source. The destination page may show what the sheet did not.
Field ruleA blank is an absence of information, not a value. Mark it not shown or unknown, and never let it stand for no, zero, none, or not applicable.
✓Baseline: the empty cell stays empty until the source page resolves it.

A worked reading

  1. Cell: blank, or a dash, or content not displayed.
  2. Reading: marked “not shown” or “unknown.”
  3. Action: re-check the destination page before any conclusion.

Field-reading example — not actual sheet data, a database, or verification.
